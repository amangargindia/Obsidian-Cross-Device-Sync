Manner of articulation:
Stops
- **Bilabial stop** — **/p/** (voiceless), **/b/** (voiced)
  - Examples: **pin** /pɪn/, **bin** /bɪn/
- **Alveolar stop** — **/t/** (voiceless), **/d/** (voiced)
  - Examples: **top** /tɒp/, **dog** /dɒɡ/
- **Velar stop** — **/k/** (voiceless), **/ɡ/** (voiced)
  - Examples: **cat** /kæt/, **go** /ɡəʊ/

Fricatives
- **Labiodental fricative** — **/f/** (voiceless), **/v/** (voiced)
  - Examples: **fan** /fæn/, **van** /væn/
- **Dental fricative** — **/θ/** (voiceless), **/ð/** (voiced)
  - Examples: **think** /θɪŋk/, **this** /ðɪs/
- **Alveolar fricative** — **/s/** (voiceless), **/z/** (voiced)
  - Examples: **sip** /sɪp/, **zip** /zɪp/
- **Palato-alveolar fricative** — **/ʃ/** (voiceless), **/ʒ/** (voiced)
  - Examples: **ship** /ʃɪp/, **measure** /ˈmɛʒə(r)/

Approximants


- A good way to describe Voiced and Unvoiced is to say SSSSS and ZZZZ out loud (do not make S voiced - it is not supposed to be made in vocals but in alveolar ridge using blade of the tongue making fricative hissing sound). In this, vocals do not vibrate. (section 1.3.0)
- Placing fingers in ears and noticing bone-conduction in voiced characters is also very effective.
