Between the two books you currently have, I would **definitely** read **_English Words: History and Structure_** first.

## Read these two in this order

### 1. ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ English Words: History and Structure (Stockwell & Minkova)

**Read first.**

Why?

- Explains **why** English words are spelled the way they are.
- Covers Latin, Greek, French, Germanic origins.
- Explains historical sound changes.
- Gives you the foundation for understanding spelling rules instead of memorizing them.

It will answer questions like:

- Why is **c** soft before **e, i, y**?
- Why is **g** sometimes /dʒ/?
- Why are there silent letters?
- Why do English words have multiple pronunciations?
- Why does spelling preserve morphology?

Since you're studying phonetics and building a structured English course, this is exactly the kind of knowledge that compounds over time.

---

### 2. ⭐⭐⭐⭐ Understanding and Teaching English Spelling (Adam Brown)

**Read second.**

This book is more practical.

It focuses on:

- teaching spelling
- common spelling rules
- helping ESL learners
- classroom strategies

It won't explain the history as deeply, but after reading Stockwell & Minkova, you'll understand *why* the rules exist, making Brown's explanations much more meaningful.

## My recommendation for your current stage

Given what you've been studying over the past few weeks, I would **not** switch away from Philip Carr yet. Instead, interleave your reading:

- **80%** of your study time: **Philip Carr** (complete it first).
- **20%** of your study time: **English Words: History and Structure** (read a chapter every few days).

This approach lets you connect what you're learning about **sounds** (e.g., /k/, /s/, palatalization, syllables) with **why English spellings encode those sounds the way they do**, reinforcing both subjects simultaneously rather than treating them as separate.
