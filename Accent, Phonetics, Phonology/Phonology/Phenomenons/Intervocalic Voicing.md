### What is it?
In **Old English**, the voiceless fricatives

- /f/
- /θ/
- /s/

became **voiced** when they occurred **between two voiced sounds** (usually vowels).

>[!definition]
>The historical sound change in which a voiceless consonant becomes voiced when it occurs between two vowels (or, more generally, between voiced sounds).

| Old English | Modern English | Change                         |
| ----------- | -------------- | ------------------------------ |
| lufu        | love           | /f/ → /v/                      |
| giefan      | give           | /f/ → /v/                      |
| hæfde       | had            | /f/ → /v/ → later lost         |
| wulf        | wolf           | No change (not between vowels) |
#### Why does it not happen in Wolf?
Because it is not between vowels. But when it becomes plural, the f becomes v
