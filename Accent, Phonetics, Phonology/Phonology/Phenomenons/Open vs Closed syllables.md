##### Open Syllable and Long vowel pronunciation

> **Rule:** When a **stressed syllable ends in a vowel** (an **open syllable**), that vowel is **usually pronounced as its long sound (its letter name).**

| Word | Syllable Division | Open Syllable | Long Vowel | IPA |
|------|-------------------|---------------|------------|-----|
| baby | ba · by | **ba** | A → /eɪ/ | /ˈbeɪ.bi/ |
| paper | pa · per | **pa** | A → /eɪ/ | /ˈpeɪ.pɚ/ |
| music | mu · sic | **mu** | U → /juː/ | /ˈmjuː.zɪk/ |
| tiger | ti · ger | **ti** | I → /aɪ/ | /ˈtaɪ.ɡɚ/ |

The **open syllable rule** is one of the strongest pronunciation rules in English.
- It applies **most reliably to stressed syllables**.
- It explains why **ba** in **baby** is /beɪ/, **pa** in **paper** is /peɪ/, and **ti** in **tiger** is /taɪ/.
- This is a **phonics rule**, unlike spelling patterns such as **ai**, **ay**, or **ei**, which are primarily orthographic conventions that must often be memorized.
- Like most English pronunciation rules, there are exceptions (e.g., *have*, *give*, *city*), but it is productive enough to help predict the pronunciation of unfamiliar words.
