[[Silent e pattern|Silent E]]
[[Pronouncing C]]
[[Pronouncing G]]

In addition to indicating that a preceding vowel is a long vowel, a silent ⟨e⟩ when it immediately follows a ⟨c⟩ or ⟨g⟩ also indicates that the ⟨c⟩ is a soft ⟨c⟩ and the ⟨g⟩ is a soft ⟨g⟩. For example: 
Măc > mācɇ 
stăg > stāgɇ

- Change

### How to prevent silent e making c and g soft - ue buffer (Plague)
If you write *plage* - g will soften and will sound like dz.

![[Recording 20260723095622.m4a]]

To prevent that from happening, we add a buffer U between the C and the E.
Plague -> G is not just before E now, so it is pronounced as Hard G.
