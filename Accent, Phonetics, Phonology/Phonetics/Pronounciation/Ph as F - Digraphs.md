[[Digraphs]]

The **ph** pairing in English is a legacy of Greek origin. When the ancient Romans adopted Greek words containing the letter **phi** (Φ), they lacked a direct equivalent in the Latin alphabet and instead opted to transliterate it as **ph**. As many Greek words related to science and philosophy, such as _phone_ and _graph_, were incorporated into English through Latin, this distinctive spelling was retained to reflect the words' ancient heritage.

The following examples illustrate the preservation of the **ph** spelling in English words derived from Greek:

| English Word | Greek Origin | Meaning |
| --- | --- | --- |
| phone | φωνή (phōnē) | voice, sound |
| graph | γράφω (graphō) | to write |
| photography | φῶς (phōs) + γράφω (graphō) | light + to write |
| philosophy | φίλος (philos) + σοφία (sophia) | love + wisdom |
| pharmacy | φάρμακον (pharmakon) | medicine, drug |

These examples demonstrate how the **ph** spelling has been preserved in English words that originated from Greek, serving as a testament to the rich linguistic legacy of the ancient Greeks.
