## Definition

**L-vocalization** is a sound change in which the consonant **/l/** (especially the **dark L** /ɫ/) loses its consonant quality and is pronounced as a **vowel** or a **semivowel** (such as /w/ or /o/).

In simple words:

> **L → vowel-like sound**

This usually happens when **/l/** occurs:
- at the **end of a syllable**, or
- before another consonant.

---

## Why does it happen?

English has two common pronunciations of **L**:

- **Light L [l]** – used before vowels.
  - *leaf* [liːf]
  - *light* [laɪt]

- **Dark L [ɫ]** – used after vowels or at the end of syllables in many accents.
  - *full* [fʊɫ]
  - *milk* [mɪɫk]

The dark L is produced with the back of the tongue raised toward the soft palate (velum). Because it already has a vowel-like quality, some dialects simplify it even further into a vowel or glide.

---

# Examples

## 1. British English (Cockney, Estuary English)

| Standard | L-vocalized |
|-----------|-------------|
| milk | miwk / mɪok |
| people | peopow |
| full | foo / fuw |
| help | hewp |
| ball | baw |

Here the **L** sounds more like **/w/** than a true consonant.

Example:

> **milk**
>
> Standard: **/mɪɫk/**
>
> L-vocalized: **/mɪwk/**

---

## 2. Australian English

Many speakers vocalize dark L.

Examples:

- milk → miwk
- school → schoow
- hill → hiw

---

## 3. New Zealand English

Examples:

- bell → bew
- well → wew
- all → aw

---

## 4. Brazilian Portuguese

Historically, syllable-final **L** became **/w/**.

Examples:

| Spelling | Pronunciation |
|----------|---------------|
| Brasil | /bɾaˈziw/ |
| mal | /maw/ |
| sol | /sɔw/ |

Although written with **L**, it is pronounced as **/w/**.

---

## 5. Historical French

Latin final **L** became a vowel.

Example:

Latin:
> alter

Old French:
> autre

The original **L** disappeared and left a vowel-like sound behind.

```

```

With L-vocalization:

```
help
/hɛwp/
```

---

# Key idea

L-vocalization does **not** delete the **L** outright.

Instead, the **L** changes from a **lateral consonant** into something **vowel-like**, such as:

- /w/
- /o/
- /u/

depending on the language or dialect.

---

# Summary

- **L-vocalization** = **/l/ → vowel or semivowel**
- Usually affects **dark L (/ɫ/)**
- Most common at the **end of a syllable**
- Found in many dialects of English (Cockney, Estuary, Australian, New Zealand) and languages like Portuguese and French.
