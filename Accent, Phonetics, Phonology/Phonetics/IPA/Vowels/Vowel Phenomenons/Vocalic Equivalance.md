 if a sound-formation is totally open then it is vowel then if we introduce a bit of constraint, it becomes an approximant consonant.


![[Broad - Open ɑː for a - Father, Palm, Calm#Vocalic equivalent between ɑ and ʕ̞]]

#### But why not call all approximants as vowel?
- **Vowels** are produced with an entirely open vocal tract. Airflow is completely laminar (smooth), with no local friction or turbulence created in the mouth or throat.
    
- **Approximants** bring two articulators (like the tongue root and the back of the throat) close enough together that they reach the threshold of **critical constriction**. If you narrow the space just a hair more, you get audible friction (a fricative).


| **Articulation Site 📍**            | **Vowel (Syllable Core) 🎵** | **Approximant (Syllable Edge) 🛑** |
| ----------------------------------- | ---------------------------- | ---------------------------------- |
| **Palatal** (Front/Hard Palate)     | [i] (like in _see_)          | [j] (like 'y' in _yes_)            |
| **Labial-Velar** (Lips/Soft Palate) | [u] (like in _too_)          | [w] (like 'w' in _win_)            |
| **Pharyngeal** (Throat)             | [ɑ] (like in _father_)       | [ʕ̞] (pharyngeal approximant)      |
