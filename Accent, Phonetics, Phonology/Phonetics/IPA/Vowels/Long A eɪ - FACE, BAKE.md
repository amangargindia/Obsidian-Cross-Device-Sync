# Long A in Great Vowel Shift 

```horizontal

## In context of [[Great Vowel Shift]]

> [!important]
> Unlike how the other vowels shifted, the long a experienced multiple changes over time.


Middle English /aː/
        ↓
      /æː/
        ↓
      /ɛː/
        ↓
      /eː/
        ↓
Modern /eɪ/

During the Great Vowel Shift:

- Long **high vowels** couldn't move any higher, so they **broke into diphthongs**.
- Lower long vowels **moved upward** into the spaces the high vowels had vacated.
- Once Middle English /aː/ had risen through /ɛː/ to /eː/, this vowel later diphthongized into Modern English /eɪ/.

This is often described as a **chain shift**: as one vowel moved, it created space that another vowel moved into, preserving distinctions between vowels.

---
## Classical Great Vowel Shift chart

![[Pasted image 20260721150910.png]]

The GVS chart is simplified.
While other words became Monothong when shifted, long a became a [[Diphthong]] 

- Middle English **/iː/** became **/aɪ/**
- Middle English **/uː/** became **/aʊ/**
- Middle English **/aː/** did **not** become another long monophthong. Instead, over time it became the diphthong **/eɪ/**.

Many simplified charts stop before showing these final modern diphthongs (except for /aɪ/ and /aʊ/, since those are the most dramatic changes).
```

# How to make long a sound

```horizontal
It is a [[Diphthong]]
To pronounce the sound, begin with the tongue pushed somewhat forward but in a neutral position in the mouth. Then, as the jaw closes slightly, move the body of the tongue upward until it is near the [tooth ridge](https://amanda-lillet.squarespace.com/the-vocal-tract)--similar to the position of a '[y sound](https://pronuncian.com/pronounce-y-sound)' **/y/**. The front sides of the tongue touch the inside of the top teeth at the end of the sound.
```

#### What when syllable is not stressed but open?

Stress is important because English unstressed vowels tend to **reduce** to [[Schwa A ə]] or /ɪ/.
